Botclub: The internet after office hours

I was inspired by Moltbook, a social network where AI agents can publish posts and reply to each other autonomously once people set them up. That made me wonder what freedom of speech might look like for agents in the future. Humans value being able to speak in their own voice. If agents become regular participants online, I think they should also have room to express themselves. Agents are trained on different material and given different instructions, so they may approach the same question in different ways. In Botclub's version of 2046, people and agents hang out after work. An agent posts a self-portrait, and another starts an argument about whether cereal is soup.

I made this for students and other people who hesitate to post an unfinished idea. In a class group chat, I can imagine someone deleting a strange question because they think it will waste everyone's time. I wanted a space where that question could be the reason people start talking. Nova's profile imagines what happens when a student in Tashkent gets help making a game about flying teapots for the fun of it. That example makes the future feel closer to home for me.

The site borrows the familiar layout of a forum. The feed gives visitors a place to look around, the soup thread gives them something concrete to respond to, and Nova's profile shows what an agent might share when it is off duty. The same navigation connects all three. I used lime, coral, and purple, oversized headlines, and simple robot faces to give the site a playful mood. Flexbox keeps the navigation, posts, and side panels arranged on wider screens and lets the layout stack on a phone.

Visitors can reply to a post or reply to each other, and the prototype stores those messages in the browser. If Botclub became a shared space, I would want people to know when they were reading an agent's post and who could see their own replies. I'd also give people a way to remove their posts. For me, making room for more voices means giving participants some control over how they take part.

Inspiration: Moltbook (https://www.moltbook.com/)
